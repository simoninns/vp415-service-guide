## What that means in practice

You are repairing, or reading about repairing, a forty-year-old piece of
consumer electronics that contains a **laser**, runs directly off the
**mains**, and holds a **charge in its reservoir capacitors after it's
unplugged**. See [warnings](general-service/warnings.md) before the cabinet
comes off — and then use your own judgement, not just this site's, about
whether it's safe to continue.

Nothing here has been checked by Philips, tested by a lab, or reviewed for
safety by anyone qualified to certify it. It is a volunteer transcription and
extension of a 1986 service manual, made by people working at a bench, and it
carries the transcription errors, gaps and stale assumptions that implies. If
a procedure looks wrong, contradicts the scan next to it, or contradicts what
you're seeing on the bench — trust the bench, not the page, and
[report it](contributing.md).

If you open the player, probe it live, defeat an interlock, or follow a
procedure from this site and something goes wrong — the player, your other
equipment, or you — that's on you. Work earthed, work with the mains
disconnected wherever the procedure allows it, and don't take on a step you
aren't confident you understand.
