# Installation

Pages 7 and 8 of the operating instructions. Where to stand the player, how to
wire it to a monitor, and which of the six Philips cables does which job.

## Siting the player

Stand the player on a firm level surface, **ensuring that the ventilation slots
on the top and sides of the player are not obstructed**. Do not stand a monitor
directly on top of the player if this obstructs these ventilation slots; in
that case a properly designed rack should be used to support the monitor. Never
stand the player directly on any electronic equipment that gives off a
substantial amount of heat, or near to any heat source. Avoid any position
where the player is subjected to direct sunlight for long periods.

## Connecting the player to the mains

The VP415 is designed to operate from a 50/60 Hz a.c. mains supply with any
voltage **between 220 and 240 V**. If your local mains supply does not fall
into this category, contact your nearest Philips organisation.

!!! danger "This apparatus must be earthed"

    The manual's warning to UK users, verbatim in substance. The wires in the
    mains lead are coloured in accordance with the following code:

    | Wire | Function | Terminal |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Green-and-yellow | Earth | Marked `E` or ⏚, or coloured green or green-and-yellow |
    | Blue | Neutral | Marked `N`, or coloured black |
    | Brown | Live | Marked `L`, or coloured red |

    These colours may not correspond with the colour markings identifying the
    terminals in your plug, which is why the table matters.

    If the player is not to be used for a long period, remove the mains plug
    from the wall socket.

## Connecting the player to a monitor

The VP415 has outputs suitable for both RGB monitors and CVBS monitors.

!!! tip "Set a monitor's VCR switch to normal"

    Some monitors have a *time-constant* switch for use with a VCR. It should
    be set to the **normal**, non-VCR position for LaserVision use.

### Euroconnector to Euroconnector

This is a direct connection, ensuring the highest quality picture and, if the
monitor is equipped for it, stereo sound. A TV receiver can be used instead of
a monitor if it has a Euroconnector socket.

Connect the cable supplied between the `A/V EUROCONNECTOR` socket on the rear
of the player and the corresponding socket on the monitor.

**This connection carries both RGB and CVBS.** Optimum picture quality is
obtained from the RGB signals, so if the monitor accepts both, make sure it is
switched to its RGB input.

### If no Euroconnector socket is available

=== "DIN AV — CVBS only"

    If the monitor is fitted with a 6-pole DIN AV (audio-video) socket, use the
    Euroconnector-to-DIN AV adaptor cable **SBC 1012** (4822 321 20485, 1.5 m).
    Connect the Euroconnector plug to the player's `A/V EUROCONNECTOR` socket
    and the DIN AV plug to the monitor.

=== "Euroconnector to BNC — CVBS only"

    Using Euroconnector-to-BNC adaptor cable **SBC 1013** (4822 321 20484,
    1.5 m), connect the Euroconnector plug to the player's `A/V EUROCONNECTOR`
    socket and one coaxial BNC plug to the video input of the monitor. Connect
    the 5-pole DIN audio plug of this adaptor cable to the audio input socket of
    the monitor, or to an audio amplifier.

=== "BNC to BNC — CVBS only"

    Using BNC-to-BNC connection cable **SBC 1015** (4822 320 11003, 1.5 m),
    connect between the player's `CVBS OUT` socket and the video input of the
    monitor. The audio signal must be taken from the player's `AUDIO OUT`
    sockets using connection cable **SBC 044** (4822 321 20344, 10 m).

!!! warning "The manual calls the same cable two different things"

    Page 7 names the BNC-to-BNC cable **SBC 1015**; Fig. 2 on page 8 draws the
    same cable, with the same service code number **4822 320 11003** and the
    same 1.50 m length, and labels it **SBC 1014**.

    One of the two is a slip and the manual does not say which. **Order by the
    service code number**, which both agree on.

### The cables, from Fig. 2

| Cable | Length | Service code | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SBC 1010 | 1.50 m | 4822 321 20486 | Euroconnector to Euroconnector, fully wired |
| SBC 1011 | 3.00 m | 4822 321 20487 | The same, longer |
| SBC 1012 | 1.50 m | 4822 321 20485 | Euroconnector to 6-pole DIN AV, 240° |
| SBC 1013 | 1.50 m | 4822 321 20484 | Euroconnector to 5-pole DIN audio (180°) and two BNC |
| SBC 1014 / 1015 | 1.50 m | 4822 320 11003 | BNC to BNC, coaxial — see the warning above |
| SBC 043 | 2.50 m | 4822 321 20308 | Twin phono to twin phono, audio |
| SBC 044 | 10.00 m | 4822 321 20344 | The same, longer |

## Connection to peripheral audio equipment

The `AUDIO OUT` sockets on the rear of the player can be connected through
connection cable SBC 043 (4822 321 20308, 2.5 m) or SBC 044 (4822 321 20344,
10 m) to a linear input of the peripheral equipment. Either or both sound
channels may be switched on or off with the `AUDIO 1` and `AUDIO 2` buttons on
the remote control handset.

If a disc contains stereo sound, this will be reproduced stereophonically when
both channels are in operation.

!!! note "Switching one channel off does not go one-sided"

    If either audio channel is switched off, the remaining audio signal is
    routed to **both** output channels. This avoids one-sided sound from a
    dual-language disc.

## Remote control handset

Normally the VP415 operates by remote control from the infra-red handset
supplied. The control buttons are grouped into logically-related sections, and
the handset controls all play functions, audio and memory controls, and access
to specified program parts.

The handset can be used with the infra-red detector on the VP415 itself or —
when a Euroconnector connection is used, and depending on the monitor — with
the infra-red detector on the monitor. **In the latter case the `RC IR/EURO`
switch on the rear of the VP415 must be set to `EURO`.**

Point the handset directly at the infra-red detector on the front of the player
or monitor. If that is not possible or not convenient, the remote control cable
supplied can be connected between the handset and the player — see
[wired remote control](introduction.md#wired-remote-control).

The handset takes **four 1.5 V batteries, type R03 or UM4**, in its base.

!!! info "The handset, taken apart"

    The service manual has the handset's exploded view, PCB and RC5 circuit on
    the [remote control page](../modules/remote-control/index.md), and its
    button codes are in
    [F-code programming](../reference/f-codes.md#handset-keys-routed-to-the-computer).
