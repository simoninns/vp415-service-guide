# Special play functions

Section 3 of the operating instructions, pages 13 to 16 — the longest section
in the book, and the one that shows what an interactive LaserVision player
could do in 1987 that a video recorder could not.

All of these functions are performed from the remote control handset. Some
behave differently depending on what the player is doing at the time, and some
work only on one type of disc; those are marked.

!!! tip "If the handset does nothing"

    Check that the `RC IR/EURO` switch on the rear of the player is in the `IR`
    position. In `EURO` the player expects handset commands to arrive through
    the monitor's Euroconnector instead.

## Standby

Pressing `STANDBY` — or the `ON/STANDBY` control on the player — during any
play operation causes the current action to cease and the player to go to
standby. Any on-screen display goes out and the `STANDBY` indicator lights. If
programming was in progress, it is terminated.

Pressing any playing mode button while the player is in standby causes the
player to commence that action.

!!! note

    If the replay function is in operation, the `STANDBY` button is disabled.

## Play, still and step

| Function | Button | Discs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Play forward** | `PLAY ▶` | Both |
| **Play reverse** | `PLAY ◀` | CAV only |
| **Still** | either half of `STILL` | CAV only |
| **Step forward / reverse** | `STILL ▶` / `STILL ◀` | CAV only — each press shows the following or preceding picture |

A still picture is useful for the close examination of a situation — in sport,
for instance — or the study of details in an instructional programme. It can
also serve as a short interlude during playback.

## The on-screen displays

Each individual picture on a CAV disc has a number encoded on the disc, and
discs may also be divided into chapters, whose numbers are encoded too.

| Press | CAV disc shows | CLV disc shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `PNR` | The current picture number, with the play mode | The elapsed time code — minutes, or minutes and seconds, depending on the disc |
| `CNR` | The chapter number | The chapter number, if the disc has chapters |

Pressing the same button again removes the display.

```
 STILL                        PLAY FWD                 PLAY FWD  TIME 11:23
 PNR   1642                   CNR   2
```

### Player and disc status

Pressing `DISPLAY` puts the player and disc status on the screen: **disc status
on the right** — CAV or CLV, side, size — and **player status on the left**.
Pressing it again switches the display off.

```
 VIDEO      ON      STEREO
 AUDIO1     ON
 AUDIO2     ON
 SLOW 1/3           CAV
 FAST 3             SIDE 1
 9600 BAUD          30 CM
```

*Fig. 7 — the player and disc status display. The baud rate line is the
quickest way to check the setting of the dip switches at the rear.*

!!! important "`DISPLAY` does something else if a number is already on screen"

    If the picture number, chapter number or time code is already displayed
    when `DISPLAY` is pressed, the player enters the **programming mode**
    instead of showing the status page. That is the door into everything under
    [programming](#programming) below.

## Search

If only part of a disc is required, it can be found quickly with the `SEARCH`
button. Pressing and holding the appropriate half moves the optical readout
unit in that direction at **approximately 20 times normal speed**, and the
screen shows a very rapid succession of pictures. When the button is released,
the player reverts to the mode it was in before.

It is often useful to have the picture number displayed while searching. On a
CAV disc **with chapter numbers displayed**, the player automatically reverts
to its previous mode when it reaches the next chapter; release `SEARCH` and
press it again to continue into that chapter. With chapter numbers *not*
displayed, searching is uninterrupted throughout the disc.

With CLV discs `SEARCH` works the same way, and the display shows elapsed time
or chapter number as appropriate.

## Pause

When `PAUSE` is pressed, both audio and video are muted and the `PAUSE`
indicator lights. Press it again to resume the previous action. Other functions
can also be started from pause by pressing the appropriate button — `PLAY`,
`STILL`, `SEARCH` and so on.

## Slow — CAV only

Forward or reverse slow motion is obtained with the `SLOW` button, and the
speed is altered with `SPEED +` and `SPEED −`:

**1/3** (default) · 1/10 · 1/25 · 1/50 · 1/100 of normal speed, normal being 25
frames per second.

## Fast — CAV only

Each press of `FAST ▶` or `FAST ◀` moves the optical readout unit at **3
(default), 10 or 20 times** normal speed, the effect on the screen being rapid
motion. The speed is set with the `SPEED` buttons.

## Audio

A LaserVision disc carries two sound channels, which can provide stereo sound
or two separate channels — a commentary in two languages, for instance.

When the player is switched on it always assumes forward play with **both sound
channels enabled**. Either can be switched off with the `AUDIO 1` and `AUDIO 2`
buttons, and the enabled channel then appears at *both* outputs. The front-panel
`AUDIO 1` and `AUDIO 2` indicators show which are on.

!!! note "Two limits worth knowing"

    - **Sound is audible in forward play only.**
    - **LV-ROM discs can carry data in place of the audio**, and where they do,
      the audio is automatically muted. On a Domesday disc that is most of the
      disc.

## Goto picture number — CAV only

1. If no picture number is displayed, press `PNR`.
2. Press the digit buttons — **maximum 5** — for the picture you want: `2 2 1
   3 5` for picture 22135. `CORR` clears a mistake so you can start again. **As
   soon as you press the first digit the player assumes still mode**, and the
   number you are entering appears below the current picture number.
3. Press `GOTO`. The player looks for the number; sound is muted and the screen
   blank during the search, with the selected number displayed. On arrival the
   picture appears in still mode, and any play mode can then be selected.

## Goto chapter number

1. If no chapter number is displayed, press `CNR`. The chapter number appears
   if the disc has one.
2. Press the digit buttons — **maximum 2** — for the chapter: `2` for chapter
   02. `CORR` clears a mistake. On a CAV disc the player assumes still mode as
   soon as the first digit is pressed.
3. Press `GOTO`. The player looks for the first picture of that chapter with
   sound muted and the screen blank, and on reaching it **starts normal play
   forward**.

## Goto time position — CLV only

1. If no time position is displayed, press `PNR`.
2. Press the digit buttons for the **minutes**, then `ENTER`. Some newer discs
   also allow seconds: enter them the same way, followed by `ENTER`. `CORR`
   clears a mistake.
3. Press `GOTO`. Sound is muted during the search and the selected time is
   displayed; on arrival, **play starts from that point**.

## Programming

A sequence of picture numbers, chapter numbers or time codes can be set up from
the handset and played back as a programme.

1. Press `PNR` (for picture number or time code programming) or `CNR` (for
   chapter programming) so that the display appears.
2. Press `DISPLAY`. The current program, if any, appears — Fig. 8.

```
 PROGRAM 1  PNR  1642            PROGRAM  CNR  2          PROGRAM 1  TIME 1:23
 1000   PLAY FWD    2000         1.CNR  5    9.CNR        12:45  PLAY FWD  17:20
 3200   FAST  10    4500         2.CNR  7   10.CNR        23:00  PLAY FWD  28:25
 2500   PLAY REV    1000         3.CNR  9   11.CNR        45:22  PLAY FWD  56:55
 51000  SLOW  50   51500         4.CNR 16   12.CNR        58:00  PLAY FWD  59:16
 2569   STILL 60    2579         5.CNR 18   13.CNR
                                 6.CNR 20   14.CNR
                                 7.CNR      15.CNR
                                 8.CNR      16.CNR
```

*Fig. 8 — the three programming displays: picture segment, chapter and time
segment.*

The player is now in programming mode. A **flashing cursor** marks the current
entry position, and the contents at that position can be changed.

### Picture segment program entry

A picture segment program may be **up to two pages long, each page of 8
segments**, one segment per line:

```
 15000 PLAY FWD 20500
```

Picture numbers are entered either directly with the digit buttons followed by
`ENTER`, or by **storing the current picture number**: when the cursor is in an
empty picture number position the player can be driven normally with `PLAY`,
`SLOW`, `FAST` and `STILL`, and pressing `ENTER` during play halts the player
and stores the picture number it stopped on. `CORR` corrects a number entered
directly.

The action — `PLAY`, `STILL` and so on — is selected by pressing the
corresponding button and then `ENTER`. Until `ENTER` is pressed the action can
be changed simply by pressing another button. Some functions take extra
information: a speed for `SLOW` (10 for 1/10 normal), and **for `STILL` a
duration in seconds, which is not optional**. `ENTER` moves the cursor to the
next position, and **if no action is entered, `PLAY` is assumed**.

| To do this | Press |
| --- | --- |
| Move to the next line | `NEXT` — from the last line it wraps to the first |
| Clear the whole program | **both `CLEAR` buttons at once** |
| Go to page 2 while page 1 is displayed | `DISPLAY` |
| End programming and return to normal play | `DISPLAY` again, from page 2 |

The program is kept by the player until it is cleared or updated.

### Chapter program entry

As above, with these differences: **up to 16 chapters** may be stored, in any
order, and a chapter may appear more than once. Chapter numbers must be entered
directly with the digit buttons; `CORR` corrects an entry.

`ENTER` and `NEXT` both move the cursor to the next line, wrapping from the
last to the first. Both `CLEAR` buttons together clear the sequence, and
`DISPLAY` ends programming. **Empty positions are ignored** when the sequence
is played.

### Time segment program entry

As for picture segments — up to 2 pages of 8 segments — in the format:

```
 time 1  PLAY FWD  time 2
```

where a time is minutes and seconds, `23:30`. Times are entered directly with
the digit buttons, or stored from play by pressing `ENTER` at the moment
wanted. **Depending on the disc, the seconds entered may be ignored.**

### Playing a programmed sequence

Press `START/REPEAT` while the picture number or time code is displayed. The
player searches for the first picture of the stored sequence and then carries
out the stored actions in order. Between items sound is muted and the screen is
blank. At the end of the last item the player **halts (CAV) or goes to pause
(CLV)**.

A sequence can be stopped by pressing any action button — `PLAY`, `FAST`,
`STILL`.

## Start/repeat

`START/REPEAT` either starts a programmed sequence — if one has been set up and
the picture number is displayed — or starts playing the entire disc from the
beginning. Pressing it again returns to the start of the disc or sequence.

## Replay

The `REPLAY` switch is at the **rear** of the player, not on the handset.

If the player is switched on with `REPLAY` on, it starts up in replay mode. If
the player is already on when the switch is turned on, play immediately starts
at the beginning of a programmed sequence, or returns to the start of the disc.

In replay mode the player is in a **continuous play loop**. If a programmed
sequence is in memory that sequence is played over and over — a picture number
or time code sequence takes priority over a chapter sequence — and if there is
no sequence, the whole disc repeats. The `REPLAY` indicator lights.

!!! warning "Replay mode disables nearly everything"

    In replay mode **all controls are disabled except**:

    - `START/REPEAT`, which moves play back to the start of the sequence or disc
    - `NEXT`, which moves play to the next segment or chapter
    - `PNR` and `CNR`
    - `AUDIO 1` and `AUDIO 2`
    - `TXT`

    To leave replay mode, press the `REPLAY` switch at the rear of the player.
    This is the mode an unattended exhibit runs in, which is why the front panel
    stops responding.

## TXT button

This button is only effective if the player is connected to a TV set with
teletext and Euroconnector remote control facilities. Pressing `TXT` switches
the monitor display between **TV mode, TXT mode and mixed mode**.
