# Service information

Chapter 8 is a Philips service bulletin bound into the manual: **VP87-01,
Survey of modifications**, dated 1987-05-18, for the LV-ROM disc drive VP415.

It is the chapter that tells you which player you are actually holding — and it
answers three questions no other chapter does: what level is each board, what
software is in each EPROM, and which known faults were fixed at which level.

| Page | Manual | What it gives you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Modification levels per module](modification-levels.md) | 167–186 | The survey of levels by production batch, then what changed at every level of every module |
| [Software releases](software-releases.md) | 187–192 | Every EPROM release with its program number, revision, checksum and service code, and what changed at each |
| [Fault symptoms](fault-symptoms.md) | 193–197 | Known symptoms, the modification that fixes each, and the level at which it landed |

## Positioning of labels

Three markings identify a player, and the sheet shows where each is:

| Marking | Where | Reads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Type number plate** | Rear panel | e.g. `TYPE 22VP 410/00`, `220V ~ 50Hz 0,4A 70W`, `PHILIPS`, `Made in Belgium`, then `AH00710 110201`. The underlined `AH00` is the **change code** and `710` the **week number** |
| **Yellow sticker** | On the assembly | A `TM:` grid numbered 1 to 18; the marked cell is the **modification level of the assembly** |
| **Module and assembly labels** | On the module and on the chassis | e.g. `VP410/00` over `0270` — the lower number is the **production number** |

The change code, week number, modification level and production number are what
you look up in the
[survey of modification levels](modification-levels.md#survey-of-modification-levels).
Where those markings are on a real player is shown on the
[rear panel photograph](../overview/controls-and-connections.md#rear).
